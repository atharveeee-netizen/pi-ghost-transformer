\section{The Engineering Problem and Physical Justification for Single-Pixel Imaging}
In high-speed textile manufacturing and surface processing, continuous webs move along conveyor lines at 1 to 2 meters per second. Microscopic anomalies (such as yarn breaks, knots, weave dropouts, and stains) degrade product quality if unspotted. Conventional automated optical inspection systems rely on multi-pixel line-scan cameras. However, this study investigates an industrial regime where Single-Pixel Imaging (SPI) with Single-Photon Avalanche Diodes (SPADs) is physically and economically advantageous over multi-pixel arrays:
\begin{enumerate}
    \setlength\itemsep{-2pt}
    \item \textbf{Cost Advantage in SWIR Wavebands:} In short-wave infrared (SWIR, 1.0 to 2.5 microns) where fabric water content, oil contamination, and internal yarn structures exhibit optimal optical contrast, 2D InGaAs focal plane arrays cost upwards of \$30,000. In contrast, a single-pixel InGaAs photodiode or SPAD costs under \$500.
    \item \textbf{Extreme Photon Starvation and Zero Readout Noise:} Under microsecond exposures, line-scan cameras receive only around 1.25 photons per pixel. With typical CMOS readout noise of 2.0 e- RMS, camera images are completely drowned in sensor noise (PSNR around 6.2 dB). SPAD single-pixel detectors count individual photon arrivals with zero readout noise.
    \item \textbf{Freezing Conveyor Motion Blur:} Fast conveyor speeds create 1D motion smear across multi-pixel exposures. Active structured illumination with microsecond pulsed modulation optically freezes target motion prior to single-bucket collection.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Our Approach: IC-PINOT Architecture and Novel Contributions}
To overcome the limitations of prior research, we developed \textbf{IC-PINOT} (In-Context Physics-Informed Neural Operator Transformer). Our framework models single-pixel computational imaging by projecting 512 orthogonal illumination patterns onto the moving target and recording total transmitted light on a single bucket detector. The neural operator fuses a physical backprojection adjoint with continuous spectral operator processing to reconstruct the target and quantify defect uncertainty in real time.

\subsection*{Novel Scientific and Engineering Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \setlength\itemsep{-2pt}
    \item \textbf{Physical Adjoint Fusion and Spectral Operator:} Rather than relying on ungrounded black-box mapping, IC-PINOT natively embeds a physical linear backprojection adjoint layer initialized with the pseudoinverse of the measurement matrix. Spectral convolution blocks in frequency space learn robust deblurring across optical defocus states, showing realistic, monotonic degradation without unphysical flat claims.
    \item \textbf{Blind Optical Inversion:} Unlike prior methods that require oracle knowledge of the true lens defocus parameter ($a_4$), IC-PINOT evaluates completely blindly from raw bucket measurements, making the framework deployable under unmonitored factory vibration.
    \item \textbf{Zero-Gradient In-Context Adaptation:} In operational textile mills, operators cannot halt production lines to collect defect labels and retrain deep models when changing fabric rolls. Our In-Context Prompt Engine conditions spatial cross-attention using reference exemplar pairs of the new weave, adapting to unseen weaves in 7.2 ms (138 FPS) without backpropagation.
    \item \textbf{Single-Pass Evidential Defect Isolation:} Conventional uncertainty methods (like Monte Carlo Dropout) require 30 stochastic forward passes per frame. IC-PINOT places an evidential Normal-Inverse-Gamma prior over pixel predictions, outputting clean reconstruction, physical sensor noise, and defect novelty in one deterministic pass at 138 FPS.
    \item \textbf{Flux-Normalized Likelihood for Single-Photon Stability:} Under extreme photon starvation (around 10 photons per pulse), standard loss functions cause severe gradient explosion. We derived a flux-normalized log-stabilized Poisson loss that aligns gradient magnitudes and ensures stable convergence.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Cross-Industrial Empirical Validation and Visual Evidence}

\textbf{Benchmark Acquisition Protocol:} All visual evaluations operate under rigorous factory conditions: sub-Nyquist compressive sampling using only 512 patterns (12.5\% compression ratio), extreme photon scarcity (mean flux around 10 photons per pulse), and 1.0-wave optical lens defocus ($a_4 = 1.0$). Inference executes in a single deterministic pass at over 100 FPS without Monte Carlo sampling.
