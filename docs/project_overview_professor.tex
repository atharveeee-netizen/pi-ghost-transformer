\section{The Engineering Problem and Physical Justification for Single-Pixel Imaging}
Automated surface inspection of continuous industrial webs requires real-time anomaly detection. Textile manufacturing lines operate at speeds between 1 and 2 meters per second. Microscopic flaws such as broken threads, knots, and holes lower product quality. 

Conventional inspection relies on multi-pixel line-scan cameras. However, this study investigates operating regimes where Single-Pixel Imaging (SPI) with Single-Photon Avalanche Diodes (SPADs) is physically or economically justified:
\begin{enumerate}
    \setlength\itemsep{-2pt}
    \item \textbf{Cost Scaling in Short-Wave Infrared (SWIR):} In the 1.0 to 2.5 micron band, fabric moisture and synthetic materials show high contrast. Multi-pixel 2D InGaAs focal plane arrays cost over \$30,000. In contrast, a single-pixel InGaAs photodiode or SPAD detector costs under \$500.
    \item \textbf{Readout Noise Floor under Ultra-Low Flux:} At microsecond camera exposure times, multi-pixel sensors receive only 1.25 photons per pixel. Conventional CMOS readout noise (2.0 e- RMS) swamps the optical signal. In contrast, SPAD bucket detectors count single photons with zero readout noise.
    \item \textbf{High-Speed Optical Modulation Bounds:} Commercial Digital Micromirror Devices (DMDs) achieve binary pattern switching rates up to 22.2 kHz. Projecting 512 patterns requires 23.0 milliseconds. We evaluate SPI under stroboscopic pulsed illumination for stationary inspection segments.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Our Approach: IC-PINOT Architecture and Novel Contributions}
IC-PINOT (In-Context Physics-Informed Neural Optical Transformer) reconstructs images from single-bucket measurements. The optical system projects 512 binary patterns onto the target. A single-pixel SPAD records bucket counts. The neural architecture reconstructs target details and estimates uncertainty in a single pass.

\begin{figure*}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.48\textwidth]{figures/architecture_diagram.png}
    \caption{\textbf{Four-stage pipeline of the proposed IC-PINOT framework.} Stage 1 performs structured illumination and SPAD bucket detection. Stage 2 executes physical adjoint backprojection and 1D transformer encoding. Stage 3 performs cross-attention and hierarchical Swin-UNet decoding. Stage 4 provides evidential uncertainty estimation.}
    \label{fig:arch}
\end{figure*}

\subsection*{Novel Scientific and Engineering Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \setlength\itemsep{-2pt}
    \item \textbf{Physical Adjoint Fusion:} The network embeds a linear backprojection layer initialized with the pseudoinverse of the measurement matrix. This layer generates a base reconstruction. Swin-UNet blocks then refine spatial details.
    \item \textbf{Blind Optical Inversion:} The model evaluates targets without oracle knowledge of lens defocus ($a_4$). It processes raw bucket signals directly, supporting operation under mechanical vibration.
    \item \textbf{In-Context Exemplar Conditioning:} The model conditions spatial cross-attention using reference exemplar pairs of new fabric rolls. This enables adaptation without test-time gradient backpropagation.
    \item \textbf{Single-Pass Evidential Uncertainty:} The decoder outputs Normal-Inverse-Gamma parameters. In one forward pass, it isolates mean reconstruction, sensor noise ($\sigma^2_a$), and defect novelty ($\sigma^2_e$).
    \item \textbf{Hardware Latency Profiling:} We report GPU tensor inference time (7.2 ms) and physical DMD acquisition time (23.0 ms at 22.2 kHz). The resulting end-to-end frame rate is 31.3 FPS.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Statistical Baseline Analysis:} Low-variance surfaces yield high PSNR under flat or brightness-only predictors. Setting all pixels to the measured mean achieves 29.22 dB on TILDA. However, flat predictors recover zero spatial texture and cannot detect structural flaws. IC-PINOT restores high-frequency defect edges and micro-weave structures, which are essential for quality control.

This indicates that cross-fabric zero-shot generalization remains an open research challenge requiring supervised adaptation.
