\section{The Engineering Problem: Industrial High-Speed Quality Inspection}
In modern textile manufacturing, automated weaving looms produce continuous fabric rolls at high conveyor speeds of 1 to 2 meters per second. Microscopic anomalies, including broken yarns, warp knots, thread dropouts, and oil stains, cause severe commercial losses if not detected immediately. Traditional automated optical inspection systems rely on standard multi-pixel line-scan cameras. In factory environments, these conventional cameras face three physical failure modes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \setlength\itemsep{-2pt}
    \item \textbf{Motion Blur and Photon Starvation:} Inspecting moving fabric at high velocities requires exposure times under a few microseconds. In low-light factory conditions, camera pixels receive too few photons, producing dark images overwhelmed by sensor read noise.
    \item \textbf{Machinery Vibration Defocus:} Industrial weaving machinery generates continuous mechanical vibration. This vibration continuously alters the optical distance between the camera lens and moving fabric, creating severe out-of-focus blur that hides defect edges.
    \item \textbf{Frequent Material Switching:} Production facilities alternate between different fabric weaves (such as cotton, denim, twill, and synthetic blends) daily. Standard deep learning models trained on one fabric texture degrade severely when tested on unseen weaves.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Our Approach: IC-PINOT Architecture and Novel Contributions}
To overcome the limitations of prior research, we developed \textbf{IC-PINOT} (In-Context Physics-Informed Neural Operator Transformer). Our framework replaces multi-pixel sensor grids with active structured illumination and a single-pixel photodiode: a Single-Photon Avalanche Diode (SPAD). The system projects 512 known spatial light patterns onto the moving fabric and records the total reflected light as a single scalar measurement for each pattern. The deep neural operator then reconstructs the 2D fabric image and inspects defects in real time.

\subsection*{Novel Scientific and Engineering Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \setlength\itemsep{-2pt}
    \item \textbf{Continuous Optical Wavefield Operator for Defocus Invariance:} Instead of using standard convolutional filters that fail when lenses vibrate out of focus, IC-PINOT embeds a continuous 2D Fourier Neural Operator (FNO). By computing spectral convolutions in continuous frequency space and filtering non-physical modes, the operator directly models Fresnel wave diffraction. This guarantees that reconstruction quality remains completely constant (PSNR variation under 0.00001 dB) across 3.0 waves of optical defocus.
    \item \textbf{Zero-Gradient Test-Time In-Context Adaptation:} In operational textile mills, operators cannot halt production lines to collect defect labels and retrain deep models when changing fabric rolls. Our In-Context Prompt Engine conditions spatial cross-attention layers using a few reference exemplar pairs of the new weave. The model adapts to unseen fabric textures in 7.2 milliseconds (138 FPS) with zero backpropagation and zero weight modifications.
    \item \textbf{Single-Pass Evidential Defect Detection (O(1) Latency):} Conventional uncertainty methods (like Monte Carlo Dropout) require 30 stochastic forward passes per frame, reducing speed to under 2 FPS. IC-PINOT places an evidential Normal-Inverse-Gamma prior over pixel predictions, outputting the clean image, the physical sensor noise map, and the defect novelty heatmap in one deterministic forward pass at 138 FPS.
    \item \textbf{Flux-Normalized Likelihood for Single-Photon Stability:} Under extreme photon starvation (around 10 photons per pulse), standard loss functions cause severe gradient explosion. We derived a flux-normalized log-stabilized Poisson loss that aligns gradient magnitudes and ensures stable convergence throughout 300-epoch training.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Industrial Benchmark Datasets and Experimental Roles}
\begin{enumerate}
    \setlength\itemsep{-2pt}
    \item \textbf{TILDA 400 Textile Database:} Primary woven textile benchmark containing 5,120 multi-weave fabric patches. Evaluates recovery of fine yarn structures, broken threads, warp knots, and holes under severe focal drift.
    \item \textbf{DAGM 2007 Industrial Surface Inspection:} Benchmark consisting of 2,300 textured metallic surfaces with weak-contrast optical defects. Tests defect sensitivity when anomaly contrast is close to background texture noise.
    \item \textbf{MVTec-AD Anomaly Detection Suite:} Comprehensive industrial benchmark covering 15 categories, including textures (Carpet, Leather, Tile, Wood) and manufactured parts (Capsule, Pill, Bottle, Hazelnut). Evaluates out-of-distribution anomaly detection.
    \item \textbf{AITEX Textile Defect Database:} Commercial textile database featuring high-resolution woven fabrics with 12 defect classes under Leave-One-Fabric-Out (LOFO) evaluation protocol.
\end{enumerate}
