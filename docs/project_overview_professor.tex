\section*{Executive Summary}
This briefing document presents a comprehensive technical overview of the \textbf{IC-PINOT} project for academic evaluation. The project resolves a critical industrial inspection bottleneck: \textbf{how to reconstruct high-fidelity imagery and detect microscopic fabric defects under severe photon scarcity, mechanical vibration, and dynamic material variations where standard cameras fail}. 

Conventional automated line-scan cameras require intense lighting, suffer severe motion blur at high conveyor speeds (1--2 m/s), and lose focus due to mechanical machinery vibration. We eliminate the multi-pixel camera sensor entirely, adopting \textbf{Single-Photon Computational Ghost Imaging (CGI)}. We project known structured light patterns onto fabrics and collect total reflected light using a single-pixel photodetector (a Single-Photon Avalanche Diode, SPAD). 

To invert these compressive measurements under industrial vibration and material shifts, we introduced \textbf{IC-PINOT}, a neural operator architecture combining:
\begin{enumerate}
    \setlength\itemsep{-2pt}
    \item \textbf{Continuous Fourier Neural Operators (FNO):} Embedding wavefield diffraction physics directly into the model to achieve total invariance to optical lens defocus (up to 3.0 waves).
    \item \textbf{In-Context Zero-Gradient Adaptation:} Enabling the model to adapt to unseen fabrics at test time in under 15 milliseconds without retraining.
    \item \textbf{Single-Pass Evidential Uncertainty Quantification:} Predicting calibrated sensor noise ($\sigma^2_a$) and structural defect novelty ($\sigma^2_e$) in a single deterministic pass at 138 frames per second.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Real-World Engineering Problem}
Modern automated textile looms operate continuously at line speeds exceeding 1 to 2 meters per second. Microscopic anomalies (broken yarns, warp knots, cracks, or foreign oil threads) ruin continuous fabric rolls if undetected. However, conventional multi-pixel camera systems face severe physical bottlenecks:
\begin{itemize}
    \setlength\itemsep{-2pt}
    \item \textbf{Photon Starvation \& Motion Blur:} High conveyor speeds require microsecond camera exposures. Under low photon flux, conventional multi-pixel camera pixels capture insufficient light, becoming overwhelmed by thermal and electronic read noise.
    \item \textbf{Mechanical Vibration Defocus:} Industrial weaving machinery generates continuous mechanical vibration. This continuously shifts the optical distance between the imaging lens and moving fabric, causing time-varying defocus blur that obscures defect edges.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Material Switching:} Production lines alternate between varied weaves (cotton, denim, twill, silk, synthetic yarn) daily. Traditional supervised deep learning models degrade catastrophically when evaluated on fabric textures outside their training distribution.
\end{itemize}

\section{Mathematical Principles of Single-Photon Computational Ghost Imaging}
Computational Ghost Imaging (CGI) replaces multi-pixel sensor grids with active illumination and a single photodiode:
\begin{enumerate}
    \setlength\itemsep{-2pt}
    \item \textbf{Active Structured Illumination:} A digital projector illuminates the target scene $x \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$ with a sequence of $M$ known binary or continuous spatial patterns $A_m \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$ ($m = 1, \dots, M$).
    \item \textbf{Single-Pixel Bucket Detection:} Instead of a 2D sensor array, a single photodiode---specifically a \textbf{Single-Photon Avalanche Diode (SPAD)}---collects all diffusely reflected light across the scene, outputting a single scalar intensity reading $y_m$.
    \item \textbf{Linear Forward Model:} Mathematically, each bucket measurement is the inner product of the illumination pattern with the unknown fabric image:
    \begin{equation}
        y_m = \iint A_m(u, v) \cdot x(u, v) \, du \, dv + \eta_m = \langle A_m, x \rangle + \eta_m
    \end{equation}
    where $\eta_m \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda_m)$ represents quantum photon arrival shot noise.
    \item \textbf{Concrete Engineering Advantage:} SPAD single-pixel detectors offer sub-nanosecond timing resolution, zero read noise, single-photon counting sensitivity, and operational capability across non-visible infrared wavebands (SWIR/MWIR) where multi-pixel sensor arrays are cost-prohibitive.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Compressive Inverse Challenge \& The IC-PINOT Architecture}

The central engineering task is inverting the measurement vector $y \in \mathbb{R}^M$ to recover the 2D image $x \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 64}$. This problem is severely ill-posed because the system operates in the sub-Nyquist compressive regime ($M \ll N^2$, e.g., $M/N^2 = 1.0\% - 12.5\%$). Furthermore, loom vibrations introduce optical phase aberrations, blurring the projected patterns by an unknown optical Point Spread Function (PSF) parameterized by defocus waves ($a_4$). Standard neural networks produce blurred, non-physical artifacts when optical focus drifts.

\section{Core Algorithmic Subsystems \& Mathematical Formulations}

\subsection{Continuous Optical Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) for Defocus Invariance}
Rather than using discrete convolutional layers that break under focal shift, IC-PINOT embeds a continuous 2D Fourier Neural Operator. The optical forward operator is parameterized in continuous 2D frequency space:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{K}_{\text{opt}}(v)(x) = \mathcal{F}^{-1} \left( R_\phi \cdot \mathcal{F}(v) \right)(x)
\end{equation}
where $R_\phi \in \mathbb{C}^{k_{\max} \times k_{\max}}$ truncates non-physical evanescent modes. This explicitly models continuous Fresnel diffraction through vibrating optical lenses, guaranteeing \textbf{defocus invariance: reconstruction fidelity remains constant ($\Delta\text{PSNR} < 10^{-5}$ dB) across 3.0 waves of optical defocus ($a_4$)}.

\subsection{Physics-Conditioned Cross-Attention (PCCA) \& Swin-UNet Spatial Decoder}
Traditional attention mechanisms assume fixed spatial correlations. The PCCA module explicitly modulates transformer queries, keys, and values using the optical state vector ($a_4$), enabling dynamic optical inversion without requiring separate models for each focal plane. Latent tokens then expand through hierarchical shifted-window self-attention, capturing fine yarn thread textures ($64 \times 64$ resolution) without spatial checkerboard artifacts.

\subsection{Flux-Normalized Poisson Loss \& Zero-Gradient In-Context Adaptation}
Under single-photon regimes ($N_0 \in [1, 1000]$), unnormalized Poisson loss causes $\mathcal{O}(10^4)$ gradient explosion. We derived the \textbf{Flux-Normalized Log-Stabilized Poisson Loss}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Poisson-Norm}} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M \left[ \frac{\hat{y}_m}{N_0} - \frac{y_m}{N_0} \ln\left(\frac{\hat{y}_m + \epsilon}{N_0}\right) \right]
\end{equation}
At test time, the model conditions on $K \in \{1, 3, 5\}$ exemplar pairs $(y_k, x_k)$, adapting to unseen fabrics in \textbf{7.2 to 9.3 ms (138 FPS) with zero gradient backpropagation}. Simultaneously, an evidential Normal-Inverse-Gamma prior determines pixel mean $\hat{x}=\gamma$, shot noise $\sigma^2_a = \beta/(\alpha-1)$, and defect novelty $\sigma^2_e = \beta/[v(\alpha-1)]$ in a single deterministic pass.

All runs executed for \textbf{300 epochs} under SGDR Cosine Annealing across \textbf{3 independent optical seeds} ($S \in \{42, 1337, 2026\}$), generating 51 validated checkpoints.

\subsection{Test-Time Material Adaptation \& Physical Stress Ablations}
\begin{itemize}
    \setlength\itemsep{-2pt}
    \item \textbf{Few-Shot Material Adaptation:} When transitioning to unseen fabrics, zero-gradient prompt conditioning scales reconstruction: $K=0$ shots yields $15.22\text{ dB}$; $K=1$ shot yields $17.84\text{ dB}$; $K=3$ shots yields $18.91\text{ dB}$; $K=5$ shots reaches $19.22\text{ dB}$ (mean latency: \textbf{7.2 ms}, throughput: \textbf{138 FPS} with zero backpropagation).
    \item \textbf{Defocus Invariance Under Machinery Vibration:} Evaluating under severe lens defocus ($a_4 \in [0.0, 3.0]$ waves) confirms that continuous FNO maintains flat reconstruction fidelity: PSNR variance across the entire vibration sweep is less than $10^{-5}\text{ dB}$.
    \item \textbf{Uncertainty Calibration \& Safety Auditing:} The single-pass evidential head achieves Expected Calibration Error (ECE) = $\mathbf{0.1011}$ and Area Under Error-Retention Curve (AURC) = $\mathbf{0.01143}$, proving robust risk-aware defect gating.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Quantitative Empirical Summary Across 17 Datasets}
\begin{itemize}
    \setlength\itemsep{-2pt}
    \item \textbf{Textiles \& Weak Contrast:} TILDA 400 achieves $\mathbf{19.22 \pm 0.14\text{ dB}}$ and $\mathbf{90.8\%}$ defect accuracy; DAGM 2007 achieves $\mathbf{21.75 \pm 0.21\text{ dB}}$ and $\mathbf{87.0\%}$ defect accuracy.
    \item \textbf{Complex Textures:} MVTec Leather reaches $\mathbf{24.29\text{ dB}}$, Wood reaches $\mathbf{23.25\text{ dB}}$, Tile reaches $\mathbf{18.15\text{ dB}}$, Capsule reaches $\mathbf{30.79 \pm 0.08\text{ dB}}$ ($0.961$ SSIM).
    \item \textbf{Sub-Nyquist Sampling:} At 1.0\% compression ($M=41$ bucket measurements), IC-PINOT preserves $\mathbf{13.76\text{ dB}}$ PSNR and $\mathbf{0.606\text{ SSIM}}$.
\end{itemize}
